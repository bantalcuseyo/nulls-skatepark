1. Mission and prime directives
Mission. Build a complete, polished, playable arcade skateboarding game in the spirit of the classic "pro skater"
score-attack formula (short timed runs, trick combos, grinds, manuals, collectibles, per-level goals, unlockable characters),
presented as moody nocturnal pixel art that matches the four attached reference images. The game must be light (low
resolution, small download) but feel like a real, finished game: menus, progression, saving, audio, juice and a satisfying
skating feel.
Prime directives

Playable first. The game must run (npm run dev) after every milestone. Never leave the repo broken.

Feel over features. Skating must feel tight, readable and fun within 10 seconds of first play. Spend time tuning.

Light and fast. 480x270 internal resolution, 60 FPS on a low-end laptop and mid-range phone, total download under 20
MB (aim for under 10 MB).

Two platforms, one game. Desktop and phone are both first-class targets with different input, layout, defaults and
performance handling (section 3.1).

Small, self-contained tasks. Work in small tasks with explicit files and acceptance checks so that smaller or cheaper
models can do most of the work (section 12.1).

Match the references. Characters and environments must look like they belong to the same world as the attached
images (sections 4 to 6).

Data-driven. Levels, tricks, characters, goals, physics values and strings live in JSON so content can be tuned without
code changes.

Original content only. Do not use the name, logo, characters, music or assets of any existing franchise (including Tony
Hawk's Pro Skater / Activision). Generic trick names (kickflip, 50-50, etc.) are fine. No copyrighted music or ripped
assets.

Autonomy with checkpoints. Work through the milestones in section 12 in order. After each: build passes, tests pass,
commit, update PROGRESS.md.
2. Game overview
Genre
2D side-view arcade skateboarding, score attack (2.5D parallax presentation).
Session
2-minute runs per level, instant retry (under 1 second). A free-skate mode without timer.
Platforms
Desktop browser and phone browser (landscape), two tuned profiles (section 3.1).
Modes
Career (goals per level, unlocks, saved progress) and Free Skate (unlocked levels, no timer, no goals).
Content scope
3 levels, 4 playable characters, about 30 tricks (flips, grabs, rotations, grinds, manuals, specials), 6 goals per
level.
Tone
Chill, nocturnal, lo-fi urban skate culture. Family friendly: no violence, no real brands, no real people.
Pillars
1 Flow: chain tricks, grinds and manuals into one big combo. 2 Risk and reward: longer air time and bigger
rotations score more but bail more. 3 Readable and tight: responsive controls, clear feedback. 4
Atmosphere: neon, dithered light cones, graffiti, great audio.
Non-goals
No online multiplayer, accounts, monetization, 3D, or realistic physics simulation.
3. Technical foundation
Area
Requirement (default unless the human overrides)
Language / engine
TypeScript + Phaser 3 (3.80 or newer), bundled with Vite. If you strongly prefer another engine (e.g.
Godot 4 web export, Kaplay), you may, but justify it in DECISIONS.md and keep every other constraint.
Targets
Modern desktop and mobile browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge). Static build in dist/ deployable to
itch.io or GitHub Pages. Fully offline single-player.

5.1 Shared design rules

In-world the skaters are called Nulls: blank-faced skaters with solid black heads. This is the identity of the game, so the
blank black head is never replaced by a face.

Anthropomorphic cat-like skaters. The entire head is a solid, flat black silhouette: no mouth, no nose, no skin tones.
Pointed ears (inner ear may have a small tan or lavender highlight).

The only expressive features are luminous eyes (white half-lidded crescents or closed-eye arcs) or lenses/visors.
Expression comes from eye shape, posture and animation, not a face.

Black gloved hands. Chibi proportions: head is about one third of total height. Baggy, oversized 90s streetwear
silhouettes. Chill, relaxed body language.

In-game sprite: about 40 to 48 px tall on a 64x64 cell, 1-px dark outline, soft warm or cool rim light on one side.

7.5 Combo and scoring

A combo is a chain of tricks, grinds, manuals and gaps linked without a bail and without more than 1.2 s of plain rolling
between links. When the link timer expires the combo banks into total score.

Combo score = (sum of trick points) x multiplier. The multiplier is the number of distinct tricks in the combo. A
repeated trick scores 50% and does not raise the multiplier.

Special trick (consumes the full meter) doubles the multiplier for the remainder of that combo.

Special meter fills from trick points (faster with long combos and clean landings; Loop fills fastest) and drains to zero on
bail.

HUD callouts: stacked trick names with running total and multiplier, e.g. KICKFLIP + INDY + 50-50 1,250 x 3.
Add "SICK!" style callouts at score thresholds (original wording).

At timer end, any combo in progress gets a short grace period and then banks automatically.
7.6 Landing, balance and bail

Landing angle relative to the surface normal: Clean within 25 deg (full points, small speed bonus), Sketchy 25 to 50
deg (combo kept, points reduced, speed loss), Bail beyond 50 deg.

A flip trick not finished at touchdown: if more than 35% of the animation remains it is a Bail, otherwise Sketchy.

Balance meter (grind and manual): value b in [-1, 1] drifts away from 0 at a rate that increases with time and is reduced
by the Balance stat. Left/Right nudges it back. The green zone (|b| < 0.35) pays points; |b| >= 1 bails. An Assist setting
widens the zone and enables rail auto-snap (default ON on phone, OFF on desktop).

Bail costs only the current combo and about 1.0 s. No lives or health. The skater respawns rolling at the last safe
ground position. Bailing off the map edge or into a bail zone (track bed) is handled the same way. The skater can never
fall out of the world.

11. Asset pipeline
You cannot rely on perfect final art from the start. Use a placeholder-first pipeline so gameplay is never blocked, and so
final art can be swapped in later without touching code.
Step
What to do
1 Tools
tools/downscale_refs.py: resample the four reference images to 480x270 (LANCZOS, then quantize
to the master palette) into public/assets/ref_scaled/. tools/extract_palette.py: produce
palette.json.
2 Placeholders
tools/make_placeholders.py generates sprite sheets with the final names, sizes and frame counts:
black-head silhouette with ears, gloves, coloured outfit blocks, simple board, so every animation exists from
day one. Also placeholder tiles, ramps, rails, props in palette colours.
3 Final art
Replace placeholders by hand-drawn or AI-generated pixel art (see Appendix A). Naming:
char_{id}_{anim}_{frame} in a Phaser JSON atlas, or fixed 64x64 grid sheets. Swapping must require
zero code changes.
4 Backgrounds
Layered PNGs per level (far / mid / near / foreground). Ground tiles 16x16 or 32x32. Use REF-ENV-2/3
downscales as first-screen mid layers and extend with mirror-tiled seams. Build the alley side-on from the kit;
use REF-ENV-1 for the title and loading art.
5 Packing
Atlas packing (max 2048x2048), no sub-pixel offsets, power-of-two optional. Report total asset size in
PROGRESS.md at each milestone.
